\chapter{Introduction background - May 6th}

Graphs have a structure and a schema. The schema itself isn't unique and challenging, but the relationships between the entities form a structure which is the interesting part of analyzing graphs. 

- Graphs are similar to any regular business model that can be represented by an RDBMS
- Structure exists in graphs, and it is sufficiently different from business data that graphs become a challenging data management problem.

Social Networks and Web-Graphs are not extracted from RDBMS, but they \emph{just are}. So whatever garbage I have written about graph extraction in the opening paragraph needs to be amended. 

Lots of data captures relationships, and we can turn them into graphs. 

Graphs have all the problems of regular data: schema, attributes, updates, real-time requirements (consistency requirements): this is the OLTP part of things
OLAP-type analysis is also possible. 

BUT graphs have structures that can best be analyzed with recursive queries.
RDBMS' have tried and failed to handle recursive queries, but have mostly failed. These types of queries are mostly what we care about. 

Graph databases give primacy to conventional data management.
Graph Processing Systems give primacy to the structural analysis.
WE: build a best-of-both-worlds system by paying attention to the structure of the graph we can build an in-between system that strikes a reasonable balance between the two non-overlapping objectives.
