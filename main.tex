\begin{abstract}
A graph database such as Neo4j can store and query property graphs but performs poorly on whole-graph analytics.
A graph analytics engine such as Ligra delivers strong analytical performance but requires a costly data-loading step and lacks transactional guarantees.
Users must choose between persistent, transactional graph storage and fast analytics, but cannot have both.
We present \project{}, a graph database built on top of \wt{}, a transactional key-value store used in production.
\project{} inherits \wt{}'s crash recovery and transactional properties without the engineering cost of building them anew.
\project{} supports both analytical and transactional property graph workloads.
Across 10 LDBC SNB queries, \project{} is $4$--$7\times$ faster than Neo4j on point lookups and aggregates and up to $4\times$ faster on full-graph analytical scans via parallel columnar reads.
Insertion throughput scales near-linearly to 72 threads, sustaining up to 1.48\,M edges/sec ($3\times$ LiveGraph), and post-ingestion graph analytics performance remains competitive with purpose-built dynamic graph systems across all kernels and scales.
On structural, static graph analytics, \project{} is competitive with in-memory graph analytics engines and outperforms out-of-core engines, while providing transactional semantics that specialized in-memory dynamic graph containers lack.
\end{abstract}
